--- PURC_SECTION: overview
Public Utilities Regulatory Commission (PURC) - Overview
PURC is the independent regulator of electricity and water utility services in Ghana, established in October 1997 under the Public Utilities Regulatory Commission Act, 1997 (Act 538). Under the Energy Commission Act, 1997 (Act 541), PURC also regulates the transportation of natural gas. PURC is not ECG - it is the independent government body that sets and approves the rates ECG and other utilities are allowed to charge, and it handles complaints if a utility does not resolve a customer's issue.
PURC is an independent body under Section 4 of Act 538, not subject to control by any authority in performing its functions, though it reports annually to Parliament. It is made up of nine Commissioners, including a Chairman, an Executive Secretary, representatives for labour, industry and domestic consumers, and four sector experts, appointed by the President for renewable five-year terms.
Mission: To build a credible and sustainable utility regulatory regime that protects stakeholders' interests.
Companies regulated by PURC include: Ghana Grid Company (GRIDCo), Ghana National Gas Company (GNGC), Electricity Company of Ghana (ECG), Northern Electricity Distribution Company (NEDCo), Enclave Power Company (EPCL), and Ghana Water Company Limited (GWCL).
Contact: PURC head office is in Accra, Ghana. Main line: 0302 218 300 / 0302 244 181. Website: purc.com.gh.

--- PURC_SECTION: functions
What PURC Does For Customers
PURC's key functions are to: set guidelines for utility rates; examine and approve the tariffs utilities like ECG can charge; protect the interests of both consumers and utility providers; monitor and enforce standards of service; promote fair competition among utilities; and receive, investigate and settle complaints relating to utility services.
If a customer has reported a billing dispute, overcharge, or service complaint to ECG and it has not been resolved at the District level, the next step is to escalate the complaint to PURC, which has the statutory power to investigate and settle disputes between customers and utility companies. Complaints can be raised through purc.com.gh or by calling PURC's Accra office on 0302 218 300.

--- PURC_SECTION: how_tariffs_are_set
How Electricity Tariffs Are Set and Reviewed
Electricity tariffs in Ghana are approved by PURC, not by ECG itself. There are two ways tariffs change: a periodic Major Tariff Review (a full review of the cost structure, done every few years), and a routine Automatic Adjustment (Indexation) Formula, published in Gazette No. 15 of 25th February 2011, which allows PURC to adjust tariffs quarterly to reflect changes in exchange rates, inflation, and fuel costs, without a full review.
Because of this, electricity, water, natural gas and net metering tariffs are typically revised every quarter (roughly every 3 months). The figures given below were the rates most recently published by PURC and are correct as of their effective date, but customers should always confirm the current rate via the ECG PowerApp (powerapp.ecg.com.gh) or purc.com.gh, since a newer quarterly gazette may have since replaced them.

Residential Electricity Tariffs (Effective 1st July 2026)
These are the End-User Tariffs (EUT) PURC approved for ECG and other distribution companies, published 16 June 2026, replacing the previous rates from Gazette No. 134 (14 April 2026). All rates are in Ghana Pesewas per kilowatt-hour (GHp/kWh); 100 GHp = GHS 1.
Residential Lifeline band (0-30 kWh per month, for low-consumption households, exclusive band): 89.9315 GHp/kWh, with a separate lower Lifeline service charge of 213.0000 GHp/month. The Lifeline band exists to keep electricity affordable for the smallest residential consumers.
Residential 0-300 kWh per month: 203.7509 GHp/kWh.
Residential 301 kWh and above per month: 269.2235 GHp/kWh.
Service charge for other (non-Lifeline) residential consumers: 1073.0886 GHp/month, billed in addition to the energy charge regardless of consumption.
These figures explain why a residential bill increases sharply once monthly usage passes 300 kWh - the rate per unit jumps from the 0-300 band to the higher 301+ band.

Special Load Tariffs (SLT) for Large Business and Industrial Customers (Effective 1st July 2026)
Customers with larger electricity demand (typically factories, large commercial premises, or those connected at medium/high voltage) are billed under Special Load Tariff (SLT) categories rather than the standard residential or non-residential bands.
SLT-LV (Low Voltage, larger low-voltage customers): energy charge 240.2104 GHp/kWh, service charge 50,000.0000 GHp/month (GHS 500/month).

--- PURC_SECTION: net_metering_tariffs
Net Metering Tariffs for Rooftop Solar / Customer-Generators (Effective 1st July 2026)
Customers who generate their own electricity (for example, rooftop solar) and are connected to ECG's network under a net metering arrangement are billed differently depending on whether they use more power than they generate (Import > Export), generate more than they use (Import < Export, earning an export credit), or balance exactly (Import = Export).
For residential net-metering customers in the 0-30 kWh Lifeline band: Import Tariff 53.9589 GHp/kWh, Export Tariff (credit for power sent back to the grid) 35.9726 GHp/kWh.
For residential net-metering customers in the 31-300 kWh band: Import Tariff 122.2505 GHp/kWh, Export Tariff 81.5004 GHp/kWh.
For residential net-metering customers using 301+ kWh: Import Tariff 161.5341 GHp/kWh, Export Tariff 107.6894 GHp/kWh.
In short: a solar customer pays the Import Tariff on power drawn from ECG, and receives a credit at the (lower) Export Tariff for surplus power sent back to the grid - the Export Tariff is intentionally lower than the Import Tariff.

--- PURC_SECTION: historical_tariff_timeline
Historical Electricity Tariff Timeline in Ghana (2000-2026)
This is a reconstruction of electricity tariff changes in Ghana since 2000, built primarily from PURC's own gazettes ("Publication of Electricity Tariffs"), PURC quarterly/major Tariff Decision Papers, and PURC's own "Trends in Electricity Tariffs (2010-2020)" research report, supplemented by news reports only where no PURC document could be found. A fully sourced, row-by-row version of this timeline (272 rows, with source document and confidence level per figure) lives in backend/data/ghana_electricity_tariffs_2000_2026.csv and backend/data/README_tariffs.md - use those files for anything needing the full granularity (per consumption-band rates, service charges, SLT categories) rather than this summary. Figures below marked with a specific GHp/kWh number are exact, gazetted figures unless stated otherwise; 2000-2009 has dates and percentages only because no gazette PDFs from that decade could be located publicly.
2000-2002: PURC (established October 1997 under Act 538) was still building up its rate-setting regime; tariff adjustments occurred in February 1998, September 1998, and May 2001. In July 2002 PURC developed a Transitional Adjustment Plan for Electricity Rate Adjustment covering 2001-2004, and in August 2002 the Government of Ghana introduced the Lifeline tariff subsidy for residential customers using 0-50 kWh/month, in line with the Ghana Poverty Reduction Strategy.
2003: The Automatic Adjustment Formula (AAF) - the mechanism that lets PURC adjust tariffs quarterly for exchange rate, fuel cost and inflation changes without a full review - was enacted in January 2003 and first applied in October 2003.
2003-2006: No major tariff adjustment occurred between November 2003 and April 2006, because the Ghana Cedi-US Dollar exchange rate was stable, rainfall (and therefore hydro generation from the Akosombo Dam) was good, and crude oil prices were relatively stable in that period. The AAF was discontinued in Q2 2006 so utilities could submit direct proposals instead; this led to a major review in April 2006 with an average increase of about 35% for both electricity and water.
2006-2009: PURC granted ECG a further tariff increase in November 2007 (exact percentage not publicly archived; ECG's revenue nearly doubled the following year). By November 2009 and into early 2010, ECG, VRA, GRIDCo and Ghana Water Company had all submitted proposals to PURC for a major upward review, citing rising costs - this is the least-documented stretch of the 2000-2026 period.
2010: PURC re-introduced the AAF during a major review, approving an average increase of 89%, effective 1 June 2010 (blended average end-user rate: Residential 17.625 GHp/kWh, Non-Residential 33.333 GHp/kWh, SLT 26.667 GHp/kWh) - one of the largest single increases in the period.
2011: March - a 17.26% AAF decrease (Residential 14.583 GHp/kWh), the lowest tariff point of the whole 2010-2020 series. September - a 7% increase (16.825 GHp/kWh). December - a further 3% increase (18.8101 GHp/kWh); the AAF itself was formally published as Gazette No. 15 on 25 February 2011.
2012: An election year - three tariff reviews (March, June, September) were conducted but resulted in no change (Residential held at 18.8101 GHp/kWh throughout).
2013: October - PURC approved a major increase of 62.72% versus September 2012 (Residential 33.3211 GHp/kWh) - the largest single percentage change recorded 2010-2020, reduced down from an initially proposed 78.9%/58.19% after nationwide protests led by the Trades Union Congress (TUC).
2014: Three AAF increases - 9.73% in January (36.5632 GHp/kWh), 12.09% in April (40.98375 GHp/kWh), and 6.54% in October (43.66165 GHp/kWh).
2015: April - a further 2.63% increase (44.8099 GHp/kWh). December - a major review added 59.2%, taking Residential to 71.3375 GHp/kWh, Non-Residential to 120.767 GHp/kWh and SLT to 91.1767 GHp/kWh - the highest residential/non-residential point of the whole 2010-2020 series.
2016-2017: An election year (2016) plus 2017 - eight tariff reviews were conducted across these two years, but none resulted in a rate change; rates stayed flat at the December 2015 levels through July 2017.
2018: March - PURC approved a major tariff reduction: -17.5% for residential and -30% for non-residential customers (Residential fell to 58.8534 GHp/kWh) - the largest single decrease recorded 2010-2020 (-22.083%).
2019: July - a major review added 11.17% (Residential 65.4267 GHp/kWh) tied to eliminating SLT maximum-demand charges (folded into the energy charge) to boost industrial competitiveness. October - a further AAF increase of 5.94% (Residential 69.3125 GHp/kWh; SLT reached its 2010-2020 peak of 132.9184 GHp/kWh here).
2020: An election year - three quarterly reviews (January, April, July) resulted in no PURC-approved change; the Government of Ghana separately subsidized electricity tariffs (up to 50% for many customers, fully for Lifeline consumers) as COVID-19 relief.
2021: No tariff changes were approved during the year.
2022: September 1 - a major review (the 2022-2025 Multi-Year Tariff Review) raised the average end-user electricity tariff by 27.15%, after ECG and NEDCo had proposed increases of 148% and 113% respectively. Exact new rates: Lifeline 41.9065 GHp/kWh, Residential 0-300 kWh 89.0422 GHp/kWh, 301-600 kWh 115.5595 GHp/kWh, 601+ kWh 128.3995 GHp/kWh; SLT-LV 132.6125, SLT-MV 100.6863, SLT-HV 105.6746 GHp/kWh.
2023: February 1 - a 29.96% across-the-board AAF increase (Lifeline to 54.4627 GHp/kWh, Residential 51-300 kWh to 115.7212 GHp/kWh), driven by a 40.25% Cedi depreciation and inflation jumping to 42.63%. September 1 - a further 4.22% increase (Lifeline 63.4792 GHp/kWh, Residential 51-300 kWh 140.5722 GHp/kWh) for residential consumers using more than 30 kWh/month.
2024: April 1 - a review that restructured the residential/SLT bands (merging SLT-MV and SLT-HV into one SLT-MV/HV category) and cut tariffs for consumers using 300 kWh and above: -6.56% average for residential, -4.98% for non-residential, -4.88% for SLT-LV (Lifeline unchanged at 63.4792 GHp/kWh); exact new per-band figures were not gazetted as machine-readable text. July 1 (Gazette No. 116) - a further adjustment (figures not directly gazetted; derived estimate: Lifeline approximately 65.67 GHp/kWh). October 1 - a 3.02% increase, with exact rates: Lifeline 67.6495 GHp/kWh, Residential 0-300 kWh 153.2683 GHp/kWh, 301+ kWh 202.5190 GHp/kWh; Non-Residential 0-300 kWh 138.3771, 301+ kWh 171.9695 GHp/kWh.
2025: January 1 - opening rates of the new 2026-2030 tariff cycle's predecessor period continued from Oct 2024 levels. May 3 - a major 14.75% increase (Lifeline to 77.6274 GHp/kWh, Residential 0-300 kWh to 175.8743 GHp/kWh) combining Q1+Q2 2025 effects plus passing on 50% of outstanding 2024 under-recovered revenue (GHS 488.42 million) to consumers, driven mainly by a 39.53% jump in VRA's bulk generation charge. July 1 - a 2.45% increase (Lifeline 79.5308 GHp/kWh). October 1 - a 1.14% increase (Lifeline 80.4389 GHp/kWh, Residential 0-300 kWh 182.2442 GHp/kWh).
2026: January 1 - opening rates of the new 2026-2030 Multi-Year Tariff Order (Lifeline 88.3713 GHp/kWh, Residential 0-300 kWh 200.2159 GHp/kWh, 301+ kWh 264.5527 GHp/kWh). April 1 - a -4.81% decrease (published as Gazette No. 134 of 14 April 2026; Lifeline down to 86.9000 GHp/kWh, Residential 0-300 kWh 196.8825 GHp/kWh), driven by a 6.78% Cedi appreciation and inflation falling from 8.00% to 4.17%; this review also introduced Ghana's first commercial EV-charging tariff. July 1 - a +3.49% increase (published 16 June 2026, revoking Gazette No. 134) taking rates to the current levels given in the "Residential Electricity Tariffs" and related sections of this knowledge base.
Overall pattern: across 2000-2026, Ghana's electricity tariffs moved through long quiet stretches (little or no change for 2-3 years at a time, e.g. 2003-2006, 2012, 2016-2017, 2020-2021 - notably, PURC's own data shows tariffs were generally held flat during election years) punctuated by large step increases (2010, 2013, 2015, 2022-2023, 2025) usually driven by cedi depreciation, fuel/gas costs, or the need to catch up on deferred cost-reflectivity, plus occasional decreases (2011, 2018, 2026) when costs eased or after public backlash.

--- PURC_SECTION: tariff_regulatory_context
Tariff Publication and Legal Basis
PURC publishes tariffs under its statutory duty in Section 19 of the Public Utilities Regulatory Commission Act, 1997 (Act 538). Each quarterly publication explicitly states which earlier gazette it revokes and replaces - for example, the 1st July 2026 tariffs (published 16 June 2026) revoked and replaced the rates published in Gazette No. 134 of 14th April 2026. This is why quoting an exact tariff figure without a date is unreliable - customers should be told the rate that applies "as of" a specific effective date, and directed to confirm the current rate for their exact billing period via ECG or purc.com.gh if precision matters (e.g. a billing dispute).
